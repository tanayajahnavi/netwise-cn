21. Differentiated Services (DiffServ)

Visual study notes explaining class-based QoS, packet marking, per-hop behaviour, and how DiffServ differs from IntServ.

Study-source note: These notes explain the standard networking concept. Match the exact terminology and depth to your prescribed Unit 2 PPT; any illustrative examples below are labelled as such.

1. Learning objectives

  • Define Differentiated Services (DiffServ).
  • Explain traffic classification, marking, and forwarding treatment.
  • Understand the DS field and Differentiated Services Code Point (DSCP).
  • Describe Per-Hop Behaviour (PHB) and traffic conditioning.
  • Compare DiffServ with Integrated Services (IntServ).

2. What is DiffServ?

Differentiated Services (DiffServ) is a network architecture for providing different levels of service to traffic classes. Instead of maintaining reservation state for every individual flow throughout the network, packets are classified into classes and receive treatment based on their markings and network policies.

Core idea: classify traffic → mark packets → apply the configured treatment to each class at routers.

DiffServ can support QoS objectives, but a marking by itself does not guarantee a particular delay, bandwidth, or loss rate. The network must configure and provision the corresponding behaviour.

3. Why use DiffServ?

  • Large networks may carry huge numbers of concurrent flows.
  • Maintaining reservation state separately for every flow can be expensive.
  • Traffic classes allow routers to handle packets in aggregates.
  • Administrators can define policies for which traffic receives higher or different priority.

4. Main components

Traffic classification

Identify packets using configured criteria, such as addresses, protocol, ports, or existing markings.

Packet marking

Set the packet's DSCP to indicate the intended forwarding class.

Traffic conditioning

At network boundaries, policies may meter, shape, police, or remark traffic to keep it within an agreed profile.

Per-Hop Behaviour (PHB)

Each router applies the configured forwarding treatment associated with the packet's class.

5. DS field and DSCP

In IP packets, the Differentiated Services field contains the Differentiated Services Code Point (DSCP), which is used to select a per-hop behaviour. DSCP is six bits wide, allowing 64 code-point values. The remaining two bits in the IPv4 Differentiated Services field are used for Explicit Congestion Notification (ECN), when ECN is used.

Important distinction: DSCP is a marking in the packet. PHB is the forwarding behaviour configured for that marking in a network. The same marking should be interpreted consistently within the relevant administrative domain.

6. Per-Hop Behaviour (PHB)

A Per-Hop Behaviour describes the externally observable packet-forwarding treatment a DiffServ node applies to a traffic aggregate. It may affect how packets are queued, scheduled, or dropped during congestion.

Common PHB categoryGeneral purposeExam point
Default PHBOrdinary forwarding treatment when no special class treatment is selected.Comparable to conventional best-effort treatment, depending on configuration.
Expedited Forwarding (EF)Designed for a low-loss, low-delay, low-jitter service when properly provisioned.Often associated with voice-like real-time traffic; it is not an automatic guarantee.
Assured Forwarding (AF)Defines classes and drop precedence to differentiate forwarding treatment and congestion-drop likelihood.Packets can receive different drop treatment under congestion.

These are standard DiffServ concepts. Learn specific DSCP values only if your syllabus or PPT requires them.

7. How DiffServ works

  1. Traffic enters a network domain. An edge router identifies the traffic using the network's policy.
  2. Packets are classified. Traffic is assigned to an appropriate aggregate or class.
  3. Packets are marked. The router sets or checks the DSCP according to policy.
  4. Traffic may be conditioned. A meter checks whether traffic matches its profile; shaping, policing, or remarking may be applied.
  5. Core routers read the marking. They apply the configured PHB, for example by using class-specific queues and scheduling.
  6. Traffic exits the domain. The next network may preserve or re-evaluate markings according to its policy.

9. Advantages and limitations

✅ Advantages

  • Scales better than per-flow reservation in many large networks.
  • Routers can operate on aggregates of traffic.
  • Supports policy-based differentiation between classes.
  • Can work across managed network domains with compatible policies.

⚠️ Limitations

  • A DSCP marking alone does not guarantee QoS.
  • Requires consistent policies and capacity planning.
  • Mis-marked traffic can abuse preferred classes unless controlled.
  • Service quality can vary across domains with different configurations.

11. Exam-ready answers

3 marks — Define DiffServ.

Differentiated Services (DiffServ) is a QoS architecture that groups packets into traffic classes. Packets are classified and marked using the DSCP field, and routers apply a configured Per-Hop Behaviour (PHB) to each class. It avoids the need to maintain reservation state for every flow in the core network.

5 marks — Explain the working of DiffServ.

  1. Traffic arrives at an edge router.
  2. The router classifies traffic according to configured rules.
  3. Packets are marked with a DSCP value.
  4. Traffic conditioning, such as policing or shaping, may enforce the traffic profile.
  5. Core routers use the marking to select the configured PHB, which determines the forwarding treatment.

12. Practice questions

Q1. Expand DiffServ and DSCP.

DiffServ: Differentiated Services. DSCP: Differentiated Services Code Point.

Q2. What does PHB mean?

Per-Hop Behaviour—the forwarding treatment applied by a router to a traffic aggregate.

Q3. How many bits are in the DSCP?

6 bits.

Q4. Why is DiffServ scalable?

It generally handles aggregates/classes instead of maintaining per-flow reservation state in every core router.

Q5. Does marking a packet with a high-priority DSCP guarantee low delay?

No. Appropriate configuration, provisioning, and policy enforcement are also required.

Q6. Give one difference between EF and AF PHBs.

EF is designed for low-delay, low-loss treatment under suitable provisioning; AF provides classes and drop precedence to differentiate forwarding and drop treatment.

13. Quick revision

  • DiffServ: class-based QoS architecture.
  • DSCP: 6-bit code point in the IP Differentiated Services field.
  • PHB: forwarding behaviour associated with a class/marking.
  • Edge router: typically classifies, marks, and conditions traffic.
  • Core router: applies configured class-based treatment.
  • EF: designed for low-delay, low-loss treatment when provisioned correctly.
  • AF: uses classes and drop precedence.
  • Main strength: scalable aggregate treatment.
  • Main caution: markings alone do not create a QoS guarantee.

📘 DiffServ details explicitly shown in the PPT

Why class-based service?

Flow-based QoS can reserve resources for individual flows, but setting up and maintaining each flow does not scale well to thousands or millions of flows. DiffServ groups packets into classes and can be applied locally by routers within an administrative domain. Packets are marked in the IPv4/IPv6 Differentiated Services field.

Expedited Forwarding (EF)

  • The PPT distinguishes regular and expedited traffic; only a limited fraction is expected to be expedited.
  • Expedited packets aim for low loss, low delay and low jitter.
  • There is still one physical output line, conceptually divided into two logical service pipes.
  • Packets are classified and marked by the sender or ingress router. If hosts mark them, the ingress router may police traffic.
  • The router may maintain expedited and regular queues, giving the expedited queue priority.

Assured Forwarding (AF)

  • Four priority classes, each with its own resources.
  • Three discard classes: low, medium and high, producing 12 combinations.
  • A traffic policer such as a token bucket assigns discard class according to burst compliance: packets within small bursts are low discard; packets exceeding small bursts are medium discard; packets exceeding large bursts are high discard.

DSCP and per-hop-behaviour terminology can be useful supplementary context, but the PPT wording to preserve is “Differentiated Services field,” “Expedited Forwarding,” and “Assured Forwarding.”
