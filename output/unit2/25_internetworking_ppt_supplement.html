Internetworking: How Networks Differ & Connect

Additional material found in Unit-02 slides 54–61. This page preserves it for completeness, but it is supplementary to the explicit 24CS510 SEE syllabus.

Scope note: The official 24CS510 syllabus lists Tunneling and Packet fragmentation in Unit 2. It does not explicitly list “How networks differ,” “How networks can be connected,” or “Internetwork Routing” in that unit. Study this page if your lecturer expects all PPT slides; prioritize syllabus-listed topics for SEE.

1. Why internetworking is needed

Networks differ in technology and scope: PAN, LAN, MAN and WAN; examples in the PPT include Ethernet, Internet over cable, fixed/mobile telephone networks, 802.11 and 802.16. The challenge is to make these unlike networks work together as an interconnected system.

2. Two basic connection approaches

Translate/convert packets

Place devices at network boundaries that translate or convert packets between network technologies.

Use a common layer

Add a layer of indirection above different networks. The slides credit Cerf and Kahn (1974) with arguing for a common layer; the proposed approach evolved into the separation of TCP and IP. IP supplies a common packet format understood by routers across many network types.

3. Devices used to connect networks

DeviceLayer / role in the PPT
Repeater and hubMove bits from one end of a wire to the other; do not understand higher layers.
Bridge and switchData-link layer; build/extend networks and connect hosts, with limited protocol translation.
RouterNetwork layer; forwards packets between networks.
GatewayUsed at a boundary to interconnect unlike systems; exact behaviour depends on gateway type.

4. Internetwork routing: two levels

Networks within an internet may use different internal routing algorithms, such as link state or distance vector. The PPT therefore presents two-level routing:

Intradomain / Interior Gateway Protocol
Routing within one network/domain
Interdomain / Exterior Gateway Protocol
Routing across networks/domains

According to the slide, intradomain routing can differ between networks, while interdomain routing uses the same protocol: Border Gateway Protocol (BGP).
