Traffic Shaping

Visual study notes on traffic smoothing, leaky bucket and token bucket algorithms, key comparisons, and exam-ready answers.

1. Meaning of traffic shaping

Traffic shaping is a traffic-management technique that regulates the rate and pattern at which packets are injected into a network. It smooths bursts so that traffic conforms to an agreed or configured traffic profile.

Core idea: Instead of allowing every burst to enter the network immediately, a shaper controls when packets are released. Depending on the implementation, packets may wait in a queue; if the queue fills, packets may be dropped.

Traffic shaping is used to reduce sudden bursts, make traffic more predictable, help meet service agreements, and reduce the chance that a burst overloads a downstream link.

2. Why shaping is needed

  • Applications may generate traffic in bursts rather than at a constant rate.
  • A burst can temporarily exceed the capacity available on a link.
  • Uncontrolled bursts may build queues, increase delay and jitter, and cause packet loss.
  • A network operator may want traffic to remain within a defined rate/burst profile.
Important distinction: Shaping does not create additional link capacity. It changes the timing of packet transmission; this can introduce waiting delay.

4. Two common traffic-shaping algorithms

Leaky Bucket

Packets enter a finite buffer. The bucket drains at a configured, approximately steady rate. If packets arrive too quickly, they wait in the buffer; if the buffer is full, arriving packets may be discarded.

  • Produces a relatively steady output rate.
  • Bursts are absorbed only up to the buffer capacity.
  • Excess traffic may be lost when the bucket overflows.

Token Bucket

Tokens are generated at a configured rate and stored up to a maximum bucket size. Sending a packet consumes tokens according to the packet's size or configured accounting unit. Stored tokens allow a limited burst.

  • Controls long-term average rate.
  • Allows bursts when tokens have accumulated.
  • Bucket capacity determines the burst allowance.
Memory aid: Leaky bucket → smoother, near-constant output. Token bucket → controlled average rate with permitted bursts.

5. Leaky bucket: working and example

  1. Packets arrive and are placed in a finite queue.
  2. The output mechanism transmits queued packets at the configured rate.
  3. If arrivals exceed the output rate temporarily, the queue grows.
  4. If the queue becomes full, additional packets may be dropped.

Illustrative example: Suppose the configured output is 2 Mb/s and traffic arrives in a short burst equivalent to 6 Mb of data. Ignoring packet overhead and other traffic, transmitting that data at 2 Mb/s takes 3 seconds. The shaper can spread the burst over time instead of sending all of it at once; the waiting time depends on packet arrival order and the queue's state.

A leaky-bucket shaper's exact delay cannot be calculated from the rate alone. We would also need packet arrival times, queue occupancy, buffer size, and the packet being considered.

6. Token bucket: working and example

  1. Tokens are added at a steady configured rate.
  2. The token bucket stores tokens up to a maximum capacity.
  3. When a packet is ready, it is transmitted if sufficient tokens are available; otherwise it waits or is handled according to policy.
  4. During an idle period, tokens can accumulate, allowing a later burst within the bucket's limits.

Illustrative example: Tokens are generated at 1 Mb/s and the bucket can hold 4 Mb worth of tokens. After a sufficiently long idle period, up to 4 Mb of traffic may be eligible for an immediate burst, subject to the implementation and link capacity. Over a longer interval, the token-generation rate constrains the average eligible traffic rate.

Key point: The token bucket does not mean the sender can transmit unlimited traffic at 1 Mb/s or that every short interval is capped at exactly 1 Mb/s. It allows a bounded burst while controlling the long-term rate.

For an exam answer, define the term used in the question first. Do not treat shaping, policing, and throttling as exact synonyms.

9. Advantages and limitations

Advantages

  • Reduces sudden bursts entering a network.
  • Makes traffic more predictable.
  • Can help enforce a service contract or configured traffic profile.
  • May reduce queue overflow and congestion caused by bursts.
  • Token bucket can preserve useful burst flexibility.

Limitations

  • Queueing can increase delay.
  • Excessive shaping can hurt delay-sensitive applications.
  • Finite buffers can still overflow and cause loss.
  • Shaping cannot increase physical link capacity.
  • Configuration must reflect traffic needs and service objectives.

10. Exam-ready answers

3 marks — Define traffic shaping

Traffic shaping is a congestion/QoS technique that controls the rate and pattern at which packets enter a network. It regulates bursty traffic to conform to a configured traffic profile. Packets may be buffered and transmitted later, which smooths traffic but can introduce delay. Leaky bucket and token bucket are common shaping algorithms.

5 marks — Explain the leaky bucket algorithm

The leaky bucket algorithm uses a finite queue and a fixed output rate. Incoming packets are placed in the queue. The queue is serviced at the configured rate, so bursts are spread over time and output becomes smoother. If the buffer fills, new packets may be discarded. It helps control burstiness, but may introduce queueing delay and packet loss during sustained overload. Draw a diagram showing bursty input → finite bucket/queue → constant-rate output.

5 marks — Explain the token bucket algorithm

Tokens are generated at a configured rate and stored up to a maximum bucket capacity. To transmit a packet, the sender or shaper must have enough tokens according to the configured packet accounting. Tokens accumulate during idle periods, so a later burst can be sent using stored tokens. The token-generation rate controls long-term traffic while bucket capacity controls the permitted burst size. If tokens are unavailable, packets may wait or be handled by a configured policy.

8–10 marks — Explain traffic shaping and compare leaky bucket and token bucket

Begin with the definition and need for shaping. Draw a block diagram of bursty input, queue/rate controller and smoothed output. Explain leaky bucket: finite packet buffer, fixed drain rate, burst smoothing and overflow drops. Explain token bucket: token generation, token storage, packet eligibility, and controlled bursts. Compare the two by mechanism, burst allowance, output pattern, rate control and limitations. Conclude that shaping regulates packet timing and may add delay; it does not create extra network capacity.

📘 SLA, policing and the two buckets

  • Data traffic is bursty; bursts can fill buffers and cause packet loss. Shaping regulates average rate and burstiness at the network edge.
  • The customer and provider can agree on a traffic pattern in a Service Level Agreement (SLA). If traffic follows that pattern, the provider promises timely delivery under the agreement.
  • Excess packets may be dropped or marked for lower priority. The PPT calls monitoring whether a flow complies with the agreed profile traffic policing.
  • Leaky bucket: variable input is stored and emitted at a fixed rate; when the bucket is full, excess input cannot be stored.
  • Token bucket: the PPT describes it as more flexible than a leaky bucket because it can allow bursts instead of forcing a constant output rate at all times. Consult the original token-bucket diagram for the exact token accounting depiction.
