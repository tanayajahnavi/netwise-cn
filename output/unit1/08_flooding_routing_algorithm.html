2. What is flooding?

Definition: Flooding is a routing technique in which each incoming packet is sent out on every outgoing link except the link on which it arrived, subject to the network's forwarding rules.

Unlike shortest-path routing, a router does not need to select just one “best” next hop. It copies and forwards the packet along multiple paths. As copies travel through the network, they may reach the destination by different routes.

Conceptual diagram: flooding deliberately creates multiple packet copies.
Simple analogy: Instead of asking one person which road to take, a message is sent down every eligible road. This can help it reach the destination despite uncertainty, but creates duplicate traffic.

3. How flooding works

  1. A router receives a packet on an incoming link.
  2. It checks the packet's forwarding rules and any duplicate-control information available.
  3. It creates copies for eligible outgoing links.
  4. It sends each copy on an outgoing link except the incoming link.
  5. Neighboring routers repeat the process, causing copies to spread through the network.
  6. The destination may receive more than one copy of the same packet.

Basic algorithm (conceptual pseudocode)

  1. When packet P arrives on link L, inspect whether it should be forwarded.
  2. If P is already known as a duplicate or has exceeded its permitted lifetime, discard it.
  3. Otherwise, record the packet identity if duplicate detection is being used.
  4. Forward a copy of P on every eligible outgoing link except L.
The duplicate and lifetime checks are controls added to make flooding practical. The core flooding rule is forwarding on all outgoing links except the arrival link.

4. How can flooding be controlled?

1. Hop count / TTL

Each packet has a limit on how many hops it can travel. Routers decrement the remaining lifetime; packets are discarded when the limit is reached.

2. Sequence numbers

A source or packet origin assigns a sequence number. Routers remember recently seen packet identities and discard copies already processed.

3. Selective flooding

A router forwards copies only on links that are considered to lead roughly toward the destination, rather than on every eligible link.

These methods reduce uncontrolled repetition. The exact set of controls emphasized in an exam should follow your lecturer's notes.

5. Advantages and disadvantages

AdvantagesDisadvantages
Simple forwarding rule.Creates many duplicate packets.
Does not require routers to compute one best route first.Consumes bandwidth and router processing capacity.
Multiple copies can provide a high chance that at least one copy reaches the destination when a path is available.Can cause congestion and waste resources if not controlled.
Can be useful in discovery or special-purpose situations where reaching a destination is more important than minimizing traffic.Without hop limits or duplicate detection, packets may circulate repeatedly.
Balanced conclusion: Flooding is simple and robust in some situations, but its high overhead makes it unsuitable as a general-purpose routing strategy without controls.

7. Practice questions

Q1. Does flooding select only one next hop?

No. It forwards copies on all eligible outgoing links except the incoming link.

Q2. Why can the destination receive duplicate packets?

Copies of the same packet travel along multiple paths and may arrive independently.

Q3. What is the role of a hop-count or TTL limit?

It limits how far a packet can travel and helps prevent copies from circulating indefinitely.

Q4. How do sequence numbers help?

Routers can identify packet copies they have already seen and discard duplicates.

Q5. What is selective flooding?

Forwarding copies only on a selected subset of outgoing links, typically those believed to lead toward the destination.

Q6. Give two disadvantages of flooding.

Duplicate traffic and high bandwidth/processing overhead; it can also contribute to congestion.

Q7. Give two advantages of flooding.

It has a simple forwarding rule and multiple copies may reach a destination via alternative paths.

⚡ One-minute revision

  • Flooding rule: send copies on all eligible outgoing links except the incoming link.
  • Copies spread along multiple paths; duplicates may arrive.
  • Controls: hop count/TTL, sequence numbers, selective flooding.
  • Advantages: simple, robust in some situations, explores multiple routes.
  • Disadvantages: duplicate packets, bandwidth/processing overhead, possible congestion.
  • Flooding is not the same as shortest-path routing: it does not select only one minimum-cost route.

Self-check 🧠

1. What is the core forwarding rule?

Forward copies on every eligible outgoing link except the link on which the packet arrived.

2. Name two controls for flooding.

Hop count/TTL and sequence numbers. Selective flooding is another method.

3. Why is flooding not usually used for all ordinary data traffic?

Because the number of packet copies can create large overhead and congestion.
